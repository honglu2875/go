# Follow-up boundaries

Finish and audit the registered final-checkpoint panels before selecting another training intervention. The inherited-rate extension already fell from the parent's 52/64 primary wins to 43/64. Both learning-rate arms exceeded the registered self-play truncation threshold. The larger KataGo diagnostic also exposes an inadequate CPU time allowance for long games; retain its failures without converting them into scored outcomes or rerunning selected openings inside the original protocol.

The next learning study should distinguish forgetting during continued online learning from the recurring endgame behavior. A frozen parent supplies a useful reference. A separately registered opponent population could mix the live policy with retained checkpoints, with balanced colors and an unchanged live-expert MCTS objective. Its behavior head should learn actual opponent choices, including weaker play, without receiving the expert's policy-improvement target. This tests robustness to coadaptation while preserving the user's distinction between choosing a strong move and predicting an opponent's move. Changing the opponent population requires a new actor/checkpoint contract; the current schedule-only fork cannot silently authorize it.

Before adding another loss, replay stored after-pass decisions and compare root policy support, terminal-pass availability and values against the exact native result. Include winning and losing pass positions separately. Passing more often is not by itself an improvement; prior endgame experiments found that most available terminal passes were losses. Any termination/resignation experiment must distinguish true scored terminal targets from inferred outcomes and missing ownership labels, and retain a randomly selected continuation control to measure false terminations.

The online control is still underusing its hosts: the audited training processes averaged only 2.63–2.79 CPU cores per host. Across roughly 387–391 seconds of loop time, each host recorded about 194–203 seconds in inference calls, 59–76 in learner calls and 71–80 in native calls. These categories include waiting and are not hardware utilization counters. Before another large training run, qualify an actor-batch/worker sweep and inspect collective/dispatch traces. Candidate batch sizes are 128, 512, 2,048 and 8,192 games per host, with explicitly registered physical-core placement. Preserve the update-to-move ratio, policy publication cadence and per-game RNG/event accounting in matched controls. A larger batch that reduces the learning budget per move is not a fair throughput improvement.

The speculative systems path remains separate: move compact-cache bookkeeping and validated-prefix metadata into Rust, measure host/device traffic, then integrate proposals with actual MCTS. Preserve both full-prefix joint and compact behavior baselines. During a multi-step dispatch, pin the relevant policy versions and make the sequential comparator use the same update/publication cadence. Delayed learner updates are an algorithmic change, not free implementation detail. Measure matched games, updates, selected events, policy quality and end-to-end throughput before claiming a useful gain or MFU improvement.

Production work still needs durable storage outside the supplied disks, dependency staging for forks on replacement hosts, streaming game retention, interruption/recovery of the continued recipe, and qualification of a current KataGo backend/checkpoint. The new continuation mechanism establishes neither those operational properties nor a strong long-running model.
