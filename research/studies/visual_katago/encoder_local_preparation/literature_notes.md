Reviewed 2026-09-13 during the fixed-data encoder study. These references motivate possible mechanisms; they do not substitute for this study's learning results.

KataGo's current documentation describes board-location transformers inside nested bottleneck blocks, with learned two-dimensional RoPE, RMSNorm attention/FFN sublayers, and spatial policy outputs. This differs from our single readout over a causal observation/action history. The documentation also lists transformer checkpoints released in August 2026. Our inference is that retaining location-specific information deserves a controlled test in the causal model. [Official architecture documentation](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo/blob/master/docs/NetworkArchitectures.md)

AlphaViT applies a shared policy MLP to the board and pass tokens, preserving the mapping from board locations to candidate moves. Its experiments cover Connect Four, Gomoku and Othello. This supports considering spatial output parameterization, but does not establish a result for Go or our history-based architecture. [AlphaViT, version 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13871v3)

ResTNet interleaves convolutional residual and transformer blocks, retaining a token for each board point. Its 9x9 Go experiment reports its best result with the RRTRRT arrangement among the tested six-block configurations. Parameters and inference costs differ across those configurations, so its reported win rates are not an equal-compute architecture comparison. It motivates testing the balance of local and global processing, with our own complete encoder/decoding accounting. [IJCAI 2025 paper, Sections 3–4 and Table 1](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/0828.pdf)

The prepared local policy intervention is deliberately small: a shared pointwise projection of the already computed CNN features adds to the existing move logits; the pass correction is zero. The new projection and bias start at zero, preserving the initial policy. There remains one policy distribution and one supervised objective. CPU tests verify exact point correspondence, gradients, causal history behavior, and unchanged default outputs/gradients. Full decoding counts include the projection, and differentiated graph counts verify that it does not recompute the encoder. It has not yet been selected, qualified on TPU, or trained.
